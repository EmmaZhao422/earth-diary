Guide

Everything Earth Diary can do, in the order you will probably need it.

1. Drop your first pin

There are four ways to put a pin on the map. Use whichever feels natural.

  • Tap the map. A small card appears for that spot. Press Pin this place.
  • Tap a place name. Zoom in until cafés, restaurants, shops, stations and parks show their names, then tap one. The pin snaps to that exact place and remembers its name.
  • Press “Drop a pin”, then tap where it happened. On a phone you can also choose Use my location to pin where you are standing.
  • Right-click on a computer, or long-press on a phone, to drop a pin instantly.
  • Start from a photo. Press “Drop a pin”, then Start from a photo — or drag photos from your computer onto the map. If the photo file remembers where it was taken, the pin lands there and the date is filled in for you. If it does not, tap the map to place it. Drop a whole batch from several places and Earth Diary offers to create one pin per place and day.

Not sure where the place is? Type a city, a landmark, a restaurant name or even coordinates like 35.6595, 139.7005 into the search box at the top.

Tip: after you drop a pin you can drag it on the map to fine-tune the position. Earth Diary also suggests named places within a short walk — tap one to snap the pin to it.

2. Fill in the memory

  • Pin character and color — pick whichever character fits the memory. Your last choice is remembered.
  • Title and date — the date can be any day in the past, so you can fill in years of history.
  • Mood — one emoji that shows up next to the memory in your timeline.
  • Diary — as long as you like.
  • Photos and videos — press the dashed tile, drag files onto the panel, or paste an image. The first one becomes the cover.
  • Tags — separate with commas. Tap a tag on any memory to see everything with that tag.

3. Look back

  • Timeline lists every memory by date. Filter by a word, a #tag or a year. When you have a memory from this same day in an earlier year, it appears at the top under “On this day”.
  • Passport counts your countries, cities, continents, photos and videos, and totals the distance between your pins in order. Tap a country to fly to your pins there.
  • Journey line draws a dotted path through your pins in the order they happened.
  • Replay my journey flies you from pin to pin in the order they happened.
  • Passport card saves an image of your map and numbers that you can share.
  • Hover a pin on a computer to peek at its cover photo, title and date.

4. Keep your diary safe

Your diary is stored in your browser on the device you are using. That keeps it private, and it also means:

  • Clearing your browser’s site data, or using a private / incognito window, removes it.
  • A phone and a laptop each have their own separate diary.
  • Some browsers, Safari in particular, may clear a site’s stored data if you have not opened the site for a while. Adding Earth Diary to your home screen and exporting backups protects you from that.

So make backups. Open Settings → Export backup to download one .zip file with every memory, photo and video. To restore it, or to move to another device or browser, open Settings → Import backup and choose the file. Importing never deletes anything: it adds what is missing and keeps the newer copy of anything that exists on both sides.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The site is supported by ads and affiliate links instead of a subscription.

Who can see my pins?

Only you. Nothing you write or attach is uploaded. The privacy page explains what the map itself needs to load from the internet.

How detailed is the map?

It uses OpenStreetMap data, which in most cities includes individual restaurants, cafés, bars, shops, hotels, museums, bus stops and footpaths. Coverage depends on local volunteers, so some regions are more complete than others.

How many photos and videos can I add?

There is no fixed limit in Earth Diary; the limit is the storage your browser allows this site on your device. Settings shows how much is in use. Long videos are large, so trim them first if space is tight.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. It works in the browser on phones and tablets, and you can add it to your home screen from the browser’s share menu so it opens like an app.

Why did my photo not land in the right place?

“Start from a photo” reads the location saved inside JPEG files. Many apps and phones remove that information when you share or upload a photo, and screenshots never have it. When there is no location in the file, Earth Diary asks you to tap the map instead.

Does it work offline?

The app itself opens offline after your first visit and your pins and diary are available. Map imagery and place search need a connection.

A place is missing or has the wrong name.

You can still pin the spot and type any name you want. To correct the map for everyone, edit the place on OpenStreetMap; changes reach the map after a while.

How do I delete everything?

Settings → Delete everything removes all pins, diary entries, photos and videos from the device.
